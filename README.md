This project analyzes electric vehicle (EV) adoption in Washington State using real registration data from the Department of Licensing. The dataset includes key attributes such as electric vehicle type (Battery Electric Vehicle or Plug-in Hybrid), model year, county, and electric range. The goal is to explore trends in EV adoption, compare BEVs and PHEVs, and assess how these vehicles are distributed across the state.


Using R, we utilized the dataset, focusing on the most relevant variables. We then visualized the data through histograms, boxplots, and scatterplots to examine the range distribution, year-to-year changes, and differences between vehicle types. Summary statistics were calculated to quantify average ranges and variability between BEVs and PHEVs. A two-sample t-test revealed no significant difference in range between the two types, while linear regression showed how model year and vehicle type affect electric range.


Findings show that BEVs dominate EV registrations and are more common in urban counties with better infrastructure. Meanwhile, PHEVs appear more evenly distributed, possibly due to their hybrid flexibility. The overall trend points to increasing EV adoption statewide, driven by government incentives, improved technology, and growing environmental awareness. This analysis supports policy and infrastructure planning for future EV expansion.
